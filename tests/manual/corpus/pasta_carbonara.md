# Pasta alla Carbonara

Carbonara is a Roman pasta dish made with just a handful of ingredients:
guanciale, eggs, Pecorino Romano cheese, black pepper, and spaghetti or
rigatoni. There is no cream in authentic carbonara. Italians are famously
protective of this rule.

## Ingredients

For four people you need:

- 400 g of spaghetti or rigatoni
- 150 g of guanciale, cut into small strips
- 4 large egg yolks and 1 whole egg
- 100 g of grated Pecorino Romano
- freshly cracked black pepper
- coarse salt for the pasta water

Guanciale is the key. Pancetta is an acceptable substitute but changes the
flavor: guanciale is cured pork jowl, pancetta is cured pork belly. Bacon is
not traditional and adds smoke the dish should not have.

## Method

Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. The water should taste like the
sea. Meanwhile, render the guanciale in a cold pan over medium heat so that
the fat melts slowly and the cubes turn crispy but not burnt. Set the pan
aside off the heat.

In a bowl, whisk the egg yolks and the whole egg with the Pecorino and a
generous amount of black pepper until you have a thick paste.

Cook the pasta until al dente. Reserve a cup of the starchy cooking water
before draining. Toss the drained pasta in the pan with the guanciale to
coat it in fat. Remove the pan from any heat source. Pour the egg mixture
over the pasta and stir quickly, adding a splash of the reserved pasta water
to loosen it. The residual heat cooks the eggs into a silky sauce. If the
pan is too hot the eggs scramble and the dish is ruined.

Serve immediately with more Pecorino and pepper on top.

## Common Mistakes

Adding cream is the most famous mistake outside of Italy. Using Parmigiano
instead of Pecorino changes the flavor significantly; Pecorino is sharper
and saltier. Cooking the eggs directly over the flame turns the sauce into
scrambled eggs. Using bacon instead of guanciale adds smoke that does not
belong.

The dish takes under fifteen minutes once the water is boiling. It is a
weeknight meal in Rome, not a special occasion.
